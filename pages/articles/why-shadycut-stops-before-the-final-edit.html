Why ShadyCut Stops Before the Final Edit

AI can save hours of editing work. That does not mean it should make every creative decision for you.

Published
September 17, 2026
Reading time
—

There is an obvious dream behind AI video editing: upload several hours of footage, let AI do everything, and get a finished video ready to publish. No reviewing a five-hour stream, no searching through gameplay, no cutting. And yes — that sounds amazing.

But the further I pushed that idea while building ShadyCut, the more I realized that a completely finished AI-generated video was not actually the product I wanted to make. At some point, removing work starts removing creative control. Deciding where to stop became one of the most important product decisions behind ShadyCut.

Where should the AI stop?

Some parts of editing take an enormous amount of time without giving you much room to be creative. Before you can shape a video, you first have to work out what you have and how it fits together. That means:

  • Reviewing hours of footage.
  • Remembering what happened earlier in the session.
  • Finding the moments that actually matter.
  • Connecting scenes with context from elsewhere in the stream.
  • Deciding what belongs in the story.
  • Checking for missing setup or payoff.
  • Building the first structure.

This is where I want ShadyCut to do the heavy lifting. It can help understand the session, find the story inside it, select relevant material, arrange the scenes, and prepare an editable first cut. Done well, that can save a shitload of hours before the creative finishing stage even begins.

The stupid “SUBSCRIBE NOW” animation matters

The later creative layer is different, because that is where the video becomes yours. Every creator and editor has little habits that viewers start to recognize. Some are deliberate; some have been around so long that nobody remembers why they started.

  • Your own transitions, including the slightly ridiculous ones.
  • The terrible subscribe animation you have used for three years.
  • Your zoom style and visual rhythm.
  • Sound effects that objectively should have died in 2018.
  • The memes and music choices that fit your sense of humour.
  • Your pacing and all the small editing habits that make a video feel like yours.

Maybe your pacing is slower than some retention guru would recommend, because that is simply how you tell stories. Maybe a weird transition makes your regular viewers laugh every time. Those are not problems for AI to fix; they are style. I do not want ShadyCut to erase them in pursuit of a supposedly better video.

I don't want ShadyCut videos to look like ShadyCut videos

If ShadyCut eventually helps thousands of creators, I do not want someone to watch a few of their videos and recognize a shared ShadyCut style. I do not want the reaction to be:

If viewers can recognize that, we failed. Automatically applying the same transitions, captions, effects, music logic, and visual treatment would gradually turn creators’ videos into different versions of the same template. They might be technically polished and efficient, but they would also be incredibly boring.

ShadyCut should help understand and structure the footage. It should not become the creator’s personality. That is the line I am deliberately trying to protect.

AI should prepare the edit, not own it

That is why ShadyCut produces an editable first cut instead of a locked AI-generated video. The goal is to remove as much of the painful preparation as possible while leaving the editor free to question and change the result. ShadyCut helps answer questions like:

  • What is this session actually about?
  • Which moments matter?
  • What needs setup?
  • What pays off later?
  • What can be removed?
  • Which scenes belong together?
  • Is context missing?
  • Where is the story dragging?

From there, the creator or editor gets to shape the finished video. The proposed cut is a starting point they can review and work with, including room to:

  • Change the pacing.
  • Move scenes around.
  • Add music.
  • Add memes.
  • Add effects.
  • Use your weird transitions.
  • Put your terrible subscribe animation back in.

Make it yours.

That separation is intentional. AI handles the preparation so you can spend more time on the decisions that make the video feel like your work.

This does not mean ShadyCut will never do more

There are additional editing tools we may add later. If creators repeatedly ask for help with a particular task and it can save meaningful time, I am open to building it. But giving an editor another useful tool is different from turning the whole process into an automatic template-video generator.

NOT: Upload footage → receive generic AI video.

BUT: Upload footage → skip hours of review and story construction → start editing from something that already makes sense.

I am interested in building tools that help people edit, with room for their own judgement and style. That is the boundary I want to keep as ShadyCut grows.

Ideally, the viewer should never know AI was involved

One of the best tests for the product is what the viewer notices in the finished video. Ideally, they follow the story and recognize the creator’s style, without thinking:

The final video is still the creator’s work. They decide how it looks, sounds, feels, and ultimately tells the story. AI did the heavy lifting underneath: understanding hours of material, finding the story, and preparing the first cut. The final creative layer belongs to the human making the video.

And I think that matters.

ShadyCut cannot invent a great stream

There is a limitation worth being clear about: ShadyCut can search for the strongest story that actually exists in the footage. It cannot invent a great stream that never happened. It does not:

  • Invent events.
  • Fabricate a payoff.
  • Create fake gameplay.
  • Manufacture a story that never happened.

Not exactly delicate, but a useful product principle. ShadyCut can find and structure the best material available, and save time getting you to the edit. The streamer still needs to give it something worth working with — preferably before we start planning the castle.

Assistance without replacing identity

I want ShadyCut to save enough time that going back to manually reviewing a six-hour stream feels painful by comparison. But I do not want those savings to come at the cost of what makes creators different from one another. Getting to the edit faster should leave more room for those differences.

The AI should remove work. It should not remove personality. It should help you find the story without deciding what your style looks like. And it should get you much closer to a finished video without pretending that the final creative decisions no longer matter.
